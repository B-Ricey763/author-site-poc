Life is the intermingling of souls. When two souls converge, both reveal something within themselves that was not visible before. A new perspective, a new angle by which the light of living is reflected. Souls delight in each other's company, for only through connection are they satiated, and when loss inevitably bares its teeth, when farewells appear suddenly and without preparation, the body must weep and the soul must grow. Though it loses its source of both light and comfort, the soul will not wither or perish. Seeds of emotion sprout within it, and they grow to fill the soul with purpose. This is the way of Fountain. One such soul lay dormant far below the great mountains of the West. Her name was Ashra Fi’Namarh. She was a Sentry.

The sableclaw turned its head nervously as it stalked into the hollow. In the pitch-black environs of the Abyss, the monster was practically invisible, but with her trained eyes Ashra could spot its long tail sweeping behind it and its strangely smooth body creeping along the rocks. She suppressed a gasp as her eyes traced its length. The spotters had not exaggerated, this one was massive.

It sniffed the ground and turned its eyeless face in a wide arc. It blended into the terrain behind it so acutely, it seemed as if the hill itself were moving. The beast was aware of her presence, of that she was certain. So long as she was the only one it noticed, all would be fine. It had nearly crossed into the middle of the hollow, step after step it approached her hiding place just above the center. It snarled quietly and continued to search for her scent.

Sweat began to drip from Ashra’s forehead. Her muscles stiffened, her joints began to ache. Her breath caught, and she found she was unable to bring it back. She felt exposed and unprotected lying prone in the dark hollow. It was more of a crater really, slopes extended a few feet above her on each side. Ashra lay in the center, waiting for the moment when her prey would be close enough to strike. Just a little closer and she would be…

The sableclaw stopped.

Her eyes widened slightly, but she remained silent. Death hung upon the slightest noise. It could smell her, it knew where she was. Her mind began to race. Why did it stop? Was it preparing to pounce? Could it hear her heartbeat? Could it sense her thoughts turning over and about each other endlessly? Sableclaws seemed to react best to sound, but sometimes it would attack even stationary, noiseless targets, for seemingly no reason. *Could* it sense her thoughts? Was that even possible?

It moved.

One paw slowly moved forward, feeling at the ground under it. Its head turned right towards Ashra. She stared into what might have been its eyes if it had any. Its mouth remained closed. It would not open until it was ready to kill.

Another step.

Even the smallest sound would set the beast off. Every second of silence was a victory to Ashra. She slowly lowered her hand and gripped the hilt of her blade. Just one more step and she would give the call. Just one more step and she would be out of this hollow, and headed back home. There was nothing to fear. As long as it took one more…

“NOW!” A voice shouted on the hill to her left. A flash of orange light illuminated the hollow, and the sableclaw screeched in anger. It jumped straight for the hill, but not before Ashra flung herself at the monster, sword pointed straight at the weak spot above its hind. She missed.

Its legs swooped above her, and she watched as it landed right in front of Pash, one of her vanguards, who held a siln torch high in the air. Before it could make another move, two spears struck on either side of the sableclaw’s torso. Ashra rushed up the side of the hill and prepared to slide under its legs while it stood there, but just as she reached it, the beast shook off the two weapons angrily, and bounded after Pash, who had begun to retreat. Ashra took a spear from Hasaf, and she launched the weapon straight at the sableclaw’s left rear leg.

The spear struck home, and the sableclaw stumbled a bit before it was able to jump at Pash. Once again it shook off the spear, its rock hard skin hardly damaged by sharpened metal.

“Light!” She yelled, and one of her men lifted the siln torch, pointing it at the sableclaw.

Another blood curdling screech rang out in the darkness, but this time the sableclaw did not pounce at the source of the light. Instead, it jumped straight for Ashra.

There was no helping it. Reluctantly she focused her mind into the launch mechanism at the soles of her feet, and jumped. She felt the beast’s claws just miss her leg as she soared high above its outstretched paw. In the air, she watched as the sableclaw convulsed rabidly, enraged by the burst of siln that had ejected from her boots. Ashra landed on the other side of the hollow, and within moments the rest of her squad gathered around her.

“Its angry.” Kahyen, her second-in-command, said dryly.

“That wasn’t exactly the intention.” Ashra replied.

The beast growled in their direction, a guttural warning that sounded like a rockslide being launched from its throat. The sableclaw would take no more abuse from their siln light. If it was wary to strike before, it certainly wasn’t now.

“Should I run for reinforcements?” One of the scouts, Rak, asked.

“No.”

Ashra watched as it slithered across the base of the hollow, a short pounce away. The beast’s tail slid across the hollow, the numerous two foot long spikes that grew out of the appendage dragged dangerously against the rocky ground. Suddenly, Ashra had an idea.

“Rak, Pash, stay here with me. The rest of you, pull back.”

“Yes, Sulo!” The six other soldiers said in unison. Four jumped backwards in a flash of orange that forced another growl out of the sableclaw, but fortunately it did not attack yet. It was clearly waiting for her to make a move, it had learned that it could not catch her off guard. Sableclaws were single-minded creatures, once it picked a target, it would usually stick to it until it was dead. Having the whole squad around her lowered that chance, but with just the three of them within striking distance, it would lock onto her.

She explained her plan to Rak and Pash, and they circled around the hollow and onto the opposite ridge, behind the sableclaw. Sure enough, it did not so much as take a glance at them, although it began waving its tail more aggressively to prevent them from getting too close. It only took a half minute for them to get into position, and the sableclaw was still growling.

A pit fell into Ashra’s stomach, but this time she was able to quell her nervousness in a slow, calm breath. *This* was where she excelled. Not hiding from her opponent, not fighting backwards while she desperately avoided its own claws. No, this was where she truly felt in control. Herself and the enemy staring eye to eye. Though the sableclaw could not see, she could tell its other senses were as honed as her own. They were the same in the way, both fighting to protect their home in the best way they knew how. She pitied the beast, but some things were beyond her control. She drew her broadsword and charged it.

The creature pounced at her, looking to intercept her halfway down the small slope, its maw agape, displaying an empty, slime-ridden hole that seemed to stretch into forever. Three sharp claws stretched out at her. Ashra dropped into a slide to avoid them, but the longest claw struck her at her cheekbone, and slashed across her nosebridge and into the forehead, just missing the eye. Before it could reach the scalp however, the claws lifted upwards, and the sableclaw flew into the air, stomach facing the inside of the hollow.

At her command, the two brothers, Rak and Pash, had launched themselves into the hollow once they saw Ashra draw her sword. Their siln-powered boots gave them the necessary boost to beat the sableclaw before it could reach Ashra. Just as she planned, they were able to grab either side of the monster’s tail, and jumped upwards, carrying it with them as best they could. Now, the sableclaw’s weak spot was exposed to the entire hollow, and Ashra found herself directly under it.

“Ahkma rezal thar.” She whispered, and she jumped straight into the beast’s lower thigh, wedging her sword deep inside of it. Ashra released her grip on the sword, and the three Fi’day used the heel of their shoes to push themselves forward, launching them from above the hollow, to the side closest to their squadmates. Ashra landed just before the other two did, and the three watched as the sableclaw fell lamely onto the ground, screeching in pain. It limped up the hollow and away from the Fi’day warriors, blood pouring from the wound in its thigh.

“It is a cruel thing we do, my friends.” Ashra said. “Ahkma rezal thar.”

“Ahkma rezal thar.” They repeated together.

The blood builds cities. The motto of the Sentries of the Abyss, the phrase that blessed their kills. It had always seemed a rather heartless thing to Ashra, but she knew it was necessary all the same. If it weren’t for the Sentries, the border cities would have been long overrun by the monsters that lurked in the darkness beyond their walls.

Still, Ashra could never delight in killing a sableclaw. They were majestic creatures, deadly, but beautiful in their own right. They were not as much a threat to the cities, in fact they tended to avoid them due to their sensitivity to siln; however, no merchant or caravan was safe as long as they lurked in the shadows of the Abyss. So, the Sentries hunted them mercilessly, and as far as they knew, the only way to kill the beasts was by striking at its thigh. A slow, agonizing death awaited all sableclaws hunted within the borders. The blood builds cities.

The three stood there, silently, and were soon joined by their four compatriots. Without another word, they began following the trail the sableclaw had left. Black, viscous blood pooled at the bottom of the hollow, and left a clear path up and away from it. They followed the trail, a squad of boots quietly tapping against the hard, stone floor. A monotone landscape of blues and blacks lay before them, a sea of rock that stretched endlessly in a world without night or day. .

Ashra often found herself wondering what more could be out there, what great wonders and mysteries had the Builders left in their centuries of experimentation on the life below Fountain. If the sun was allowed in for just one day, what might she see? Murals of ancient gods long forgotten, struck down by the three rulers of this world? Perhaps ruins of civilization, unknown and unheard of by historians both on the surface and under it. Personally, Ashra hoped that she would find a living society , one undetected by the exploration parties sometimes sent out by the border cities. She could learn their language, study their ways, and talk to their leaders. She would discover what they thought being a warrior meant, how they defended themselves from the monsters that plagued her own people. She was aware it was all a fantasy, but like the best of them, it was one she often found herself in.

After a few more minutes of tracking, they finally reached the dead corpse of the sableclaw. It had taken a wretchedly long amount of time to bleed out, its long body lying sprawled across the stone. Reverently, Ashra pulled her blade from the beast’s thigh, and turned around.

“It’s far enough from Namarrah, the scavengers shouldn’t bother us.”

Her squad muttered sullenly in agreement, and they began making the long trek back to their city. The ever-silent Raiza dashed ahead, her feet moving silently over the walks, hardly causing a sign of her departure. She was the more capable of the two scouts on Ashra’s squad, but Rak was faster and often acted as their messenger. Pash, his brother walked with his head held low, a spear lamely hanging off his shoulder. He covered Ashra’s right flank, while to the left, Kahyen walked merrily as ever, though she could tell by the way his body was tense, he was much more nervous than he let on. Behind him trailed Falamin, their medic, and Hasaf, who kept his spear tight to his chest.

One medic, two scouts, two vanguard, and two officers, the standard composition of a Sentry squad. Seven proud soldiers of the Fi’day, each one of them frightened beyond belief. All except Raiza, anyways, Ashra wasn’t sure if she was capable of fear. Either way, they had fought well, and none of them were hurt. Well, none except her. Blood was dripping down her face after all. That reminded her…

“Wait!” She said.

All of them stopped at once, Hasaf, Pash, Rak, and Kahyen drawing their weapons. Raiza’s head peaked out from above a hill just to the North, her sword at the ready.

“Fal, I might need some stitching.” Ashra said wearily as she felt the giant claw mark on her face. The wound began to burn, angrily, like a brand had suddenly been pushed into her face. She sat on the ground and watched Falamin pull out a small siln lantern.

“Builders, Ashra!” Falamin exclaimed, quickly pulling out a sewing kit from her pack, “Why didn’t you tell me sooner!”

“I hadn’t noticed.” Ashra said.

“Well I should have! With all the blood from the sableclaw, I couldn’t even tell you were bleeding. That and this darkness, of course. We should be carrying torches more often!”

“And attract more beasts? Our task is hard enough as it is, don’t you– OW!”

Falamin began rubbing siln paste into the wound. It stung more than Ashra had expected, and she had to keep herself from itching at it as the attractive forces of the siln began to pull her skin together.

“How long, Fal?” Kahyen asked.

“Just a few minutes. It's shallow, but she’s bled much more than she should have. I’m examining all of you before we take another step towards Namarrah!”

Kahyen nodded, and gestured for the other three soldiers to establish a perimeter. Raiza, Hasaf, and Rak made their way across the dark landscape, and Kahyen followed shortly after, though he didn’t move far from their impromptu triage. Pash, however, lingered next to Ashra.

“I’m sorry, Sulo.” He said, his voice shaking in the darkness. “If I hadn’t called the ambush so early, you…”

“None of that, Pash.” Ashra said dismissively.He had given the signal just a step too early, which was what had caused Ashra to initially miss the killing blow on the sableclaw. There was nothing to be done about it now, however.

“You made a mistake, yes,” She said, gritting her teeth as Falamin began stitching the bottom of her cheekbone, “but I am not dead, and no one else was injured. If the only thing to come from this is a little scar from– Falamin can you be a bit more gentle!” She interjected.

“How?” The medic asked, holding a needle in her hand.

Ashra sighed.

“If the only thing to come from this is a scar and a dead sableclaw, then I’m happy.”

“We could all be dead though.” Rak’s voice came a few feet away, and soon he appeared in the lamplight. “If your plan hadn’t worked, Ashra, we’d all be dead. Don’t allow my brother grace for something that was his fault. If he had just done his job, we would be well on our way back home right now.”

“Not right now, Rak.” Ashra said, watching as the two brothers glared at each other from across the lamp.

“Yes, I messed up,” Pash said, “but I’d like to see you do any better. That’s what you want isn’t it. You want my spear, take it!”

“I don’t want your spear! I just want you to admit that you messed up, that you aren’t the warrior you always make yourself out to be!” Rak replied, raising his voice.

“What do you think I was just doing?” Pash shouted.

“Ever since we joined Ashra’s squad, you’ve been acting like you're the eldest simply because you're part of the vanguard, and I’m just a scout…”

“This is a conversation for the barracks, not for the middle of the Abyss!” Ashra yelled.

“Stop moving!” Falamin shrieked.

“AHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH”

A bloodcurdling screech burst from the area just south of them. Two orange dots lit up and launched towards it.

“That was Hasaf!” Ashra said. The two brothers turned immediately and jumped to the south as well. Ashra began to stand up, but Falamin pushed her back down.

“Your stitches aren’t finished.” She said, “I’ll go check on Hasaf, but you have to stay here.”

“Am I bleeding?” Ashra asked.

“No.”

“Then I’m fine.”

Ashra stood up, and siln lit up her boots as she began a jump of her own. She felt the miniscule orange particles crawl up her skin and into her mind, pushing into the holes in her ears and nose, resting on the front of her forehead. Though connecting took mere seconds, the space between Hasaf’s scream and the siln reaching her mind seemed to take an eternity. Eventually the magic within the gears in her armor pierced her thoughts, and she leaped away from an exasperated Falamin.

Air slammed into Ashra’s face and her long black hair whipped behind her as she soared towards Hasaf. The darkness of the Abyss enveloped her vision, but five orange dots of light guided her forward. Four were her soldiers, Pash and Rak just ahead of her, Kahyen and Raiza a jump or two farther. The fifth was presumably Hasaf, the outline of his boots bouncing up and down as if they were being carried.

*‘A shek, most likely’*

The highly intelligent beasts had the strongest eyesight of any monster within the Abyss. They would often kidnap solitary travellers, grabbing them with huge, muscular arms, and take them back to their dens for a meal. Ashra mentally pushed herself forward by forcing siln out of the back of her boots, propelling her past all four airborne soldiers. Each followed suit, and after another landing and jump sequence, they found themselves directly above Hasaf’s glowing boots.

Just as she suspected, a large shek held Hasaf tightly against its chest. Twice as large as even the tallest Fi’day, the beast resembled the apes that roamed the southern jungles on the surface, burly and dark haired. Sheks, however, were considerably bigger.

Ashra landed a few strides ahead of the shek, her landing slowed by a quick burst of siln from her boots, and she drew her sword, staring down the monster. The shek growled angrily as the rest of her squad dropped to ground, creating a circle around Hasaf’s captor. Its stance and throaty snarls spoke of a much more familiar adversary compared to the sableclaw before. One by one, the soldiers lit up their chest armor, creating a ring of orange light around the pair. Hasaf hung limp in the shek’s arms, his face a contortion of pain and anger.

Ashra watched the beast for a moment, and motioned her squad to close in on it. Sheks were much easier to kill than most other monsters in the Abyss, their fur and soft skin were unique among any animal found within Fountain’s recesses. Though what they lost in defense, they more than made up for with their sharp mind and keen intelligence. When Ashra’s mother fought with the Sentry, sheks had been the most prominent pest the soldiers had been sent to exterminate. Their dens had sat much closer to the border cities in years past, and it was primarily the former generation who had put a stop to that. Now, sableclaws were the predominant issue, but seeing a shek every few patrols was hardly uncommon.

“Halt.” Ashra called. There was something moving in the darkness.

Four pairs of white, bloodshot eyes appeared in Ashra’s periphery. They waded through the darkness slowly, but with intent, surrounding the circle of Fi’day before they could so much as jump away. The eyes floated high above the ground, not quite as tall as the shek holding Hasaf, but still considerably taller than any one of the sentries. The four newcomers waited just outside the ring of late emanating from the squad’s armor.

“Kahyen.” She called, gesturing to the hidden sheks with her sword.

“Damn.” He responded after turning towards them.

Rak and his brother, who had found and picked up Hasaf’s spear at some point, continued to close in on the original shek, pointing the tips of their weapons at its neck. The shek began to squeeze Hasaf, a pained whimper was forced out of his diaphragm. The brothers backed off once they heard Hasaf’s ribs beginning to crack, and for one, breathless moment, the entire group stood still.

Ashra inhaled the stiff, dry air of the Abyss. This air, these people, even the enemies that surrounded her, they all made up the only land she had ever known since she entered this world. It was a chaotic and cruel life, one that most would not envy. Living in constant fear of what stalked the darkness outside of her city pushed her to become a light no one would ever see, living within the shadow of her parents forced her to guide that light in others as a leader. Yet it was infinitely short moments like this that made her wonder if she was ever meant to carry the torch. Whatever happened next would occur regardless of her actions.

Before she could make a decision, one was made for her.

Seven orange lights appeared directly above them and fell upon the four surrounding sheks. The one directly to Ashra’s right fell with a spear sticking out of the front of its throat, another took a broadsword to the chest, and retreated into the darkness. Without waiting to watch the fate of the other two, Ashra activated the siln in her feet and sword. In the fraction of a second it took for the launch mechanism to begin, she angled her body towards the shek holding Hasaf and pointed her sword straight at its head. Ashra launched at the beast before it could react to the chaos around it, and her sword found its mark. The tip sliced straight through the monster’s scalp and into its brain, and it took most of it with her as Ashra landed a few feet away. The beast’s body fell limp, and the two remaining sheks retreated.

Ashra sprinted to the shek’s corpse and untangled Hasaf from its arms. He wheezed heavily, and his armor had warped under the pressure of the shek’s hold. Falamin soon appeared next to her.

“Let me, Sulo.”

“He needs his armor off.” Ashra said, reaching for the straps that held up his chest plate.

“Someone else can do it.” Falamin muttered calmly, gesturing at Ashra’s hands.

Blood and brain matter covered both gloves, and Ashra backed away nervously, wiping them on the dead beast’s fur. Someone touched her shoulder, and she jumped, ready to pull out her sword.

“Calm down Sulo.” Kahyen stood beside her. He had always teased her for her jumpiness, but there was no mischief in his eyes today.

“Are you alright?” He asked after a moment.

Ashra took a breath, rubbed the remaining grime off her hands, and nodded.

“Yes.” She said.

“Good,” He replied, “I haven’t seen you that scared since our first patrol.”

“It was nothing, Kahyen. Just a tense situation, that’s all.”

“Tense because of the sheks,” and he smiled, “or tense because Hasaf was the one in danger?”

Ashra glared at him, but allowed herself to smile at the ribbing.

“I would be tense if any of our squad was in danger, not just Hasaf.” She turned her gaze towards her injured friend nonetheless. “Let’s go see, Fal. I know that’s what *you* want to do anyways.”

Kahyen was about to retort, but Ashra was already halfway towards their second triage of the day before he could. Falamin and the medic from the reinforcement squad were examining his clearly broken ribs.

“How is he?” Ashra asked, forcing her voice to stay steady.

“Its not as bad as it looks.” The new medic responded. “I think we’ll need a moment though.”

Falamin nodded in agreement, but said nothing.

Once again, Kahyen and Ashra gave out orders for a perimeter, and soon the remaining nine soldiers were out on watch. The two medics and Hasaf remained at the site of the battle, along with the leaders of the two squads.

Ashra glared at her fellow Sulo, who had been waiting patiently near the shek he had impaled once his squad landed. Out of the other six Sulos that patrolled with the Sentry in Namarrah, Muzei Fi’Namarh was Ashra’s least favorite. Petty, rebellious, and arrogant, he was Ashra’s opposite in nearly every single way. And unfortunately…

“We’re even.” He told her in smug triumph.

“We didn’t ask for reinforcements.” She replied bitterly.

“Well, you certainly needed them.” He paused. “How is Hasaf?”

“Falamin and your medic seem confident, but we won’t be able to leave for a while.”

Ashra stared nervously at the darkness around them. Between all the clamor they made fighting and the residual siln released from their armor, it would be a wonder if every beast within the region didn’t know they were here. Still, most were smart enough not to take on thirteen healthy Sentry. That did not stop Ashra from keeping a wary hand near her blade.

“I must admit, that was a nice maneuver you used to take down the shek.” Muzei continued, “You may want to change your bandage, though.”

Ashra had, once again, forgotten about her injury. She lightly felt at the strip of cloth tied across her forehead, a bit of blood coming out as she did. Whether it was her own, or the shek’s, she wasn’t sure. In all likelihood, it was both, but that was hardly the most pressing concern.

“I appreciate the rescue, Muzei,” She said, “but why did you bring your squad here?”

“Honestly, Ashra, I am not sure.” He nervously glanced in the direction of Namarrah. “Something is going on in the city. The Sulin ordered all squads back into the city. Yours was the only one still on patrol, so we were sent to retrieve you.”

“Could the Yosul be returning?” She asked.

“I am not so sure. I think it has something to do with the revolts out east.”

“Well, it will have to wait until Hasaf is ready to be transported back.”

Muzei nodded and walked to the triage.

It was only a matter of time before the conflict in Eastern Fi’dastra reached the political solitude of the border cities. No one in Namarrah was positive what had occurred, only that there were battles in Yozar and Razarrah, and that the winning side was making its way west. If news finally had reached Namarrah, it must be of significant importance if all seven Sentry squads were recalled.

Anticipation began to grow once again, the same kind that had taken over Ashra when she was waiting for the sableclaw to strike. She had walked into two traps today, both willingly, and she couldn’t shake the feeling that she was walking into a third. The thought stayed with her as she went to go check on her injured friend once again, and it still had not left when she walked through the city gates, hours later.
