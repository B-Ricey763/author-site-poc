There had never been a war in Fi’dastra.

When Taim, the First and Only Emperor, had united the cities and made himself the ruler of all Fi’day there was no war. During the Cursed Years, when the Republic’s leadership had all fallen to the scourge of the Deep Sickness, there was no war. Even when the Ishorathi had called for the aid of their underground neighbors, when the militias of the Fifteen Cities marched in unison towards the surface in defense of their human allies, even then there was no war, for the battles were fought outside the expanse of the Abyss.

The wars of the past were fought by Fi’day whose names had long been forgotten, whose actions had no impact on the society built to hide their legacy. This society had no war, it knew no war. The Fi’day were a people of strong souls with golden speech and minds as keen as diamonds. There had never been a war amongst them.

“I am happy you agreed to speak with me, Shadenaf.” A voice rang from the gilded mouth of Manetem Fi’Razarrah, the current head of the Holy Senate, Speaker of all Fi’day. “I am sure we can come to a peaceful solution to all of this.”

Shadenaf suppressed a scowl. They were already far beyond peace.

“Soulution implies certainty, and I fear there is little we can be certain of these days.” He replied bitterly. “But please, share your plans, and I shall share mine.”

The old senator swallowed and scratched at his silver beard nervously. The bristly spikes of hair blended perfectly with his skin, as if they hid atop him, and he within them. He sat crookedly on an ancient wooden chair in front of a stone desk carved with the sigils of the Fi’day government.

The entire room was adorned with tokens of the Republic. A flag bearing the emblem of the capital, Razarrah, a painting of each of the Head Senators that preceded Manetem, Taim’s glowing hands lighting their portraits with the signature orange hue of siln, even an old treaty hung in a glass case, declaring the city-state of Ishorath and the nation of Fi’dastra eternal allies. Manetem’s office was more a historical archive than an active seat of power, a reflection of the way he treated his own post.

“Well,” Manetem began, “first I would like to get in contact with some of the human merchants who have expressed interest in our siln. They have approached some of the other senators and…”

“Yes, they approached me as well,” Shadenaf interrupted dismissively, “and  we will be lucky if we can get even half of the revenue the Ishorathi government had given us. The other merchant companies cannot make up for the loss of the old deal. ”

“They do not need to. So long as we can hold on to some form of income from our siln deposits, the country will survive.”

“How can you know that? Our people are running out of food, our cities are running out of space, and you are telling the Senate to trust in lost saviors and invisible promises. Why should anyone believe we can survive without the Ishorathi deal? ”

“Because…” He removed his spectacles and rubbed his eyes wearily. The man had looked so much older of late. He was suffering. Shadenaf pitied him.

“Because,” Manetem continued, “Ishorath *will* reconsider.”

Shadenaf leaned forward. He had not expected that. If Manetem could guarantee Ishorath’s compliance, there would be no need for further action . Maybe Shadenaf’s efforts had been pointless. Maybe he had underestimated the old senator. Maybe…

“If the Ishorathi intend to purchase all their siln from the Linay, then let them. Soon they will realize that hauling silks and other fragile goods all the way to the Well is far simpler than importing wagons of scrip into Ishorath. The siln will not hold, and they will realize their folly. They will be forced to turn back to us, and we can markup our own siln higher than we ever had before. All we need is patience.”

*‘And maybe not.’*

Shadenaf sighed. Manetem actually looked proud of himself. He truly believed that if the entirety of Fi’dastra merely sat down and waited, their income would magically reappear. That brief glimmer of hope had disappeared. This was more than just siln. The Fi’day would fall far behind the humans if the leadership was left the way it was. That *could not* be allowed to happen.

“That is certainly an interesting plan, Speaker.” Shadenaf said. “As Senator of Yozar, it would be unseemly to vote against your motion.”

“I am glad you see it that way, my friend.” Manetem smiled.

“Unfortunately, I must resign as senator. My seat on the Holy Senate is now empty.”

Manetem blinked and put his spectacles back on, as if that would help him process what he had just heard. He frowned, a million wrinkles creasing across his silver forehead.

“Shadenaf, you cannot be serious?”

“I am.”

“But why? You spent so much time campaigning, so much energy and effort into leading your city. You can’t just throw it away because you disagree with my plan. Vote against me, damnit, but don’t quit!”

“You misunderstand me, Speaker. I am not resigning because of the endless contempt I hold for the idiotic fumbling you call ‘a plan’ nor for the years of my life you have wasted as a poor excuse for a Speaker of the Fi’day. No, I am resigning because of the respect I hold for the Holy Senate. Never in the near eight centuries of our government has a senator murdered another senator, and I would hardly like that to end on its last day as an institution.”

“Shadenaf, what do you…”

But Manetem was never able to finish his sentence. With a swift strike, Shadenaf Fi’Yozar plunged his once-hidden knife into Manetem’s skull, and the Speaker’s body dropped onto the table.

The bodyguards standing in the room made no motion. It had taken much preparation, but nearly every Fi’day soldier in the Senate building was currently in on the conspiracy. The next few hours would determine whether this coup would succeed. At the very least, however, Shadenaf could take solace in the fact that new leadership would be installed.

“Bring me the Yosul.” He told one of the guards.

After a few moments, the guard returned with a tall, armored Fi’day general.

“The Senate is secured, all those who did not comply have been killed.” He said bluntly. Eishan Fi’Razarrah was not versed in the ways of subtlety, but Shadenaf was fine with that. Sometimes one needs a knife, and sometimes one needs a hammer. In that way, Shadenaf and Eishan complemented each other.

He smiled at his brusk, violent retinue. “Good.” Shadenaf said. “If all goes well, both Yozar and Razarrah will be ours by the end of the night. Take the senator’s bodies and display them on the square, like we planned.”

“Of course, my Emperor.” Eishan bowed low and waved for Manetem’s former guard to follow. Shadenaf stopped him.

“Not Emperor yet.” He said. “There is work to be done. Get those bodies displayed and ready the golems. We must be on the move before tomorrow.”

Eishan nodded and half the soldiers left the room, carrying Manetem’s body with them. Shadenaf stared at the pool of blood left on the table. Yes, much work to be done indeed.

There had never been a war in Fi’dastra. Not until today, and it was a war Shadenaf must win.
