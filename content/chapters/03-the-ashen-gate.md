The pass took two days to reach on a good horse, and Mira did not have a good horse.

She had a borrowed mule, a satchel of surveyor's tools, three days of bread, and a copy of the first map hidden inside her coat where the road-wardens would not think to look — if road-wardens stopped mapmakers, which, until that week, they never had.

On the second evening she crested the last ridge and saw the Gate.

It was not grand. That was the worst of it. Two standing stones, a lintel cracked clean through, and a thin grey haze beyond it that did not move with the wind. The ash wastes, the old songs said. The country of the dead, said the priests, who charged money to say it.

Mira took out her compass to note the bearing for her chart.

The needle did not point north.

It pointed at the Gate, steady as a held breath — and somewhere beyond the haze, very faint and very far away, something that might have been a bell began to ring.
