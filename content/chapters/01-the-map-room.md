Mira had worked in the map room for three years, and in three years no road had ever moved.

Roads, her master liked to say, were the one honest thing in the world. Rivers lied. Borders lied, usually on purpose. But a road went where it went, and a mapmaker's job was simply to write it down before someone else wrote it down wrong.

So when she unrolled the survey of the northern valleys that morning and found the King's Road a full finger's width east of where she had inked it the day before, she did what any careful apprentice would do.

She blamed herself.

She re-measured. She checked the compass rose, the scale bar, the surveyor's notes in their crabbed northern hand. Everything agreed with yesterday's map — except the road.

> "A map is a promise," Master Odo said from the doorway, not looking up from his tea. "Break one, and you'd best know why."

Mira said nothing. But that night, long after the lamps were out, she came back with a candle and a ruler, and she watched the ink.

It moved.
